How we evaluate NBA draft picks.

Two different questions need two different scores: how good was the player's NBA career, and how much value did the team receive relative to the overall pick?

Historical coverage

The NBA dataset contains 1,602 draft selections from 2000 through 2026, both rounds, including players who never appeared in an NBA regular-season game. Career totals and advanced impact data come from a 2000–2025 NBA draft history aggregation incorporating NBA Stats and Basketball-Reference-derived records. Career information is currently complete through the 2025–26 NBA season, with 2026–27 not yet finalized.

Original draft team refers to the selecting team; a draft-night trade may transfer player rights to another franchise. Career statistics include points, rebounds, assists, games, minutes, steals, blocks, per-game averages, career Win Shares, Win Shares per 48, VORP and BPM. Missing advanced impact statistics are not evidence of a zero-value career.

Career quality (0–100)

Quality is independent of draft slot. Each statistical metric is compared against the 2000–2017 drafted-player reference cohort, including players with no NBA appearance. The current descriptive model applies:

ComponentWeight
Career Win Shares (WS), percentile35%
Career Value Over Replacement Player (VORP), percentile24%
Career minutes, log-scaled percentile17%
Career Box Plus/Minus (BPM), percentile, minutes-adjusted8%
Bounded achievement / honors index16%

The honors index gives larger credit to MVP, Finals MVP and All-NBA First Team than to All-Star selections or regular-season team membership with the eventual champion. Honor credit is capped so team achievements cannot overwhelm sustained individual production. This is a model of observed career accomplishments, not a causal measure of player skill or an NBA prediction system.

Career pace (0–100)

Career pace is a descriptive comparison of annualized Win Shares, VORP, playing time, BPM, and individual achievements against players within the same completed-season tenure range: rookies, sophomores, years 3–4, 5–7, 8–10, or 11+. For recent players, we benchmark their observed performance against career-stage peers rather than mature careers. This uses career-aggregate impact data and does not reconstruct full historic season-by-season advanced metrics. It is not a future-performance prediction.

Draft value and grade (A+ through F)

The expected quality of a pick is empirically fit using the mature 2000–2017 cohorts, split into overall-pick bands: 1–3, 4–10, 11–20, 21–30, 31–45 and 46–60. The bands provide regularized priors for a smoothed, pick-specific curve constrained to nonincreasing expectations across later draft positions. For mature careers, a draft-value score is derived from 50 + 1.35 × (quality − expected), clipped to 0–100, then mapped to letter grades. Provisional grades instead use the career-pace score and a peer-based expected pace for the draft position, allowing only positive early grades. Thus a late second-rounder can earn an A with a less accomplished career than a top-three pick.

Career maturity: draft classes 2024–2026 have no letter grades; 2022–2023 only receive provisional positive grades. More recent classes may have preliminary quality measurements, but short careers must not be interpreted as busts.

Accolades and known limitations

  • MVP, Defensive Player of the Year, Finals MVP and Rookie of the Year: winner seasons 2000–2026. These are NBA annual award winner ledgers, not all vote recipients.
  • All-NBA: first, second and third team selections, 2000–2026, matched by player and draft chronology. Tied or name-ambiguous matches remain unassigned rather than guessed.
  • All-Defensive: first- and second-team selections are recorded from 2000–2026, including tie years, based on the NBA's official historic honor roll and contemporary season-level selections. Award names that cannot be unambiguously tied to a player draft record remain unassigned.
  • All-Star: available career selection counts from a Wikipedia-derived history; the dataset does not provide a verified per-season All-Star list and therefore no seasons are invented.
  • NBA titles: the displayed count is a regular-season appearance for a team that ultimately won the championship that season, NOT an independent count of championship rings, Finals appearances or postseason roster eligibility.
  • 2026 draft: selection records and prospect positions are included. The 2026–27 NBA season is in progress, so these rookies are ungraded.

Backtest and data quality

On the 2018–2020 draft-class retrospective holdout (180 players), the empirical pick-band model yielded RMSE 18.58, compared with 20.89 from a constant reference mean. These are v1 historical benchmarks, not a validation of the new v2 smoothed model or a forecast of the future. Full source checksums, identity checks, match audits, computation and private model code are held in the private research repository. The website publishes only allowlisted derived player-level results.
