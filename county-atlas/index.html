Important: These are ecological, cross-sectional county associations, not evidence of causation or individual behavior. Armed Forces residents (% ages 16+) is not annual Army enlistment or recruiting rates. Rates using 2020 population are population-normalized proxies from different dataset years. SEDA performance cannot be plotted unless authorized scores are published. Spatial clustering, bases, age differences, selection effects and outliers can influence results.

Demographic shares are county aggregates and must not be interpreted as individual characteristics or predictive explanations of crime. CDC PLACES health metrics are modeled; mortality metrics are resident death rates, not police-recorded incidents. Sources span different years and reporting standards. Avoid causal interpretations.

Regression coefficients are descriptive standardized ordinary least squares on complete paired counties. Optional controls and interactions are county-level, unweighted by default. The interaction term estimates whether the X–Y slope varies with a third metric; it does not prove that metric causes the variation.

THE RESEARCH / SOURCE NOTES

What the map measures.

01 / RELIGION

Congregations, not buildings

“Congregations” refers to congregations reported by participating religious groups in the 2020 U.S. Religion Census, not just Christian churches. The figures cover multiple faith traditions and do not represent a complete directory of church buildings. Some groups do not report.

U.S. Religion Census ↗
02 / SCHOOLS

Official K–12 school locations

NCES Common Core of Data 2024–25 public schools and Private School Survey 2023–24 private schools. Historical directories can include schools whose operational status has since changed.

NCES EDGE ↗
03 / LOCAL DIRECTORY

Records are not interchangeable

School names and addresses come from NCES. Worship listings, when available, come from DHS/HIFLD's geocoded nonprofit inventory and may be incomplete or outdated. They are not used to calculate the congregation map.

DHS HIFLD ↗
04 / GEOGRAPHY

Rates, ratios and county boundaries

Population rates divide each count by 2020 population, then multiply by 10,000. The two ratios are (public + private schools) ÷ reported congregations × 100 and reported congregations ÷ (public + private schools) × 100. Population normalization does not apply to either, so the rate control is disabled for both. National and filtered ratios use pooled totals, not averages of county ratios. Small denominators can produce extreme values; a zero denominator or missing count makes a county's ratio undefined. Boundaries use US Atlas Census-derived geography. Historical FIPS changes can cause unmatched counties.

US Atlas geography ↗

Education: High-school diploma and bachelor's attainment measure adults age 25+, not current students, school performance, test scores or four-year cohort graduation rates. Stanford SEDA 6.0 provides historical county-level public-school achievement estimates in grades 3–8 for 2009–2019. SEDA map variables remain disabled until publication permission and a verified data extract are available; these measures are not current test scores, private-school quality, or student graduation rates. Military: Civilian veterans are former military personnel; Armed Forces residents measure the population serving at the time of the ACS survey. Neither measure captures the annual count or percentage of people newly enlisting. Business: CBP small establishments have 1–4 paid employees, nonemployers have no paid employees, BDS establishment births are newly opened employer locations, and self-employment includes incorporated and unincorporated workers. These measures are different, and not equivalent to firm startup counts. ACS five-year estimates represent 2020–24; suppressed and missing values are not treated as zero. For percentages, aggregate summaries pool county numerators and denominators. The household-income summary is a median of county medians, not the national median income.

Coverage: the 50 states and District of Columbia in the map. Alaska and Hawaii are included. U.S. territories are not visualized here. Missing reports are shown as missing—not as zero. Counts are not a guarantee of every currently active location. No Google Places data are harvested or stored.

09 / SCHOOL PERFORMANCE

Stanford SEDA 6.0 · grades 3–8

Historical county achievement estimates pooled across 2009–2019, overall and by math and reading. Cohort-standardized scores are shown in standard-deviation units (below or above national reference), never as school star ratings or graduation rates. This layer is not yet available: Stanford's data-use agreement prohibits publishing data files in full or part without explicit permission. We will not publish county values until permission is verified and an approved export passes validation.

10 / DEMOGRAPHICS

Race & Hispanic ethnicity

2024 ACS five-year B03002 county estimates. Hispanic/Latino includes people of any race; the seven race categories are non-Hispanic, mutually exclusive with Hispanic ethnicity. These are county population shares, not person-level characteristics or causal predictors.

ACS B03002 data dictionary ↗
11 / PUBLIC HEALTH

CDC PLACES 2025

County-level crude adult prevalence estimates. CDC models these using surveys and Census variables; they are not direct counts of medical diagnoses. Health-related social-needs measures do not cover every state. Missing measures are withheld, and national summaries show medians of reported counties, not national prevalence.

PLACES 2025 county data ↗
12 / FATAL VIOLENCE & INJURY

CDC mortality by residence

Annual NVSS homicide, firearm homicide, suicide, overdose and total firearm death rates per 100,000 residents. Source year is selected coherently by release. Small-county rates may use Bayesian smoothing to protect privacy. Not interchangeable with FBI offenses, violent crime incidence, or police use-of-force events.

CDC injury and violence rates ↗
13 / REPORTED CRIME

Violent crime & juvenile delinquency

2024 County Health Rankings analytic archive includes violent offenses reported by participating law-enforcement agencies, normalized per 100,000, and juvenile delinquency court cases per 1,000 youth. The underlying incident/court-case years can be older than 2024; nonparticipating locations are not treated as zero. These are not measures of the same event or affected population.

2024 CHR analytic archive ↗
14 / RESEARCH GAPS

Police violence, crimes, kidnapping & pornography usage

Archived violent crime and juvenile delinquency court-case rates can be displayed where the 2024 County Health Rankings national extract reports them. These are not current 2026 incident counts, and juvenile delinquency does not mean children were victims. FBI crime clearances, kidnappings, and crimes involving children as victims require an independently validated law-enforcement reporting crosswalk and coverage. National county-level police brutality measures lack a consistent official definition. No credible, representative public county pornography-consumption dataset has been verified. None of these are shown as invented or zero-valued layers.
